Hello

I'm Santosh. This is my notebook.

Nebula is a personal blog about the craft of building software — the parts I find joyful, the parts I find frustrating, and the questions I'm still circling around.

It is written slowly, edited honestly, and shipped from a small studio with too many monitors.

A short history

I started Nebula in late 2021, somewhere between two jobs I was tired of and one I hadn't started yet. The premise was selfish: I wanted somewhere to think out loud about software without the pressure of a brand voice, a content calendar, or an editor with a quota. Five years later that is still mostly the premise.

Most weeks I publish one essay. Some weeks I publish nothing. A few times a year I write something long enough that I have to admit, sheepishly, that it's an essay and not a note.

What you'll find here

I write about engineering (mostly the backend kind), design (mostly type and color), the strange middle ground where AI is changing how we work, and — increasingly — the slow, weird joy of building tools just for myself. Occasionally I publish a piece about teaching, hiring, or the particular shape of working remotely from India.

What you won't find

Sponsored posts, affiliate links, lifecycle emails, AI-generated filler, or anything that involves the phrase "10x your productivity." I keep no analytics beyond a server log I read once a quarter. Your inbox stays uncluttered because I don't have anything to sell you.

The stack

Nebula is hand-rolled HTML, a sprinkling of Tailwind, and a Three.js scene or two. Hosted on a small VPS with a static file server. No CMS, no JS framework, no analytics, no cookies. I do everything in a text editor that has not received a feature update in three years, and I am grateful for that.
